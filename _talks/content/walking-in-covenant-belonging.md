Brothers and sisters, it is a joy to worship together today. When we gather in sacrament meeting, we renew our sacred covenants[^1] and remember the Savior Jesus Christ.

Over the past few months, I have pondered what it truly means to experience "covenant belonging." Elder Gong reminded us that covenant belonging unites families and connects us intimately to heaven[^2]. It is not a passive status, but an active, daily partnership with God.

In Alma chapter 37, we learn that by small and simple things are great things brought to pass[^3]. When life feels overwhelming, we do not need to perform heroic feats every morning. Rather, our consistent devotion—morning prayer, scripture reflection, and quiet moments of repentance—anchors our soul.

President Nelson taught that making and keeping covenants actually makes our lives easier, not harder[^4]. When we yoke ourselves to the Savior, His grace carries the heaviest part of our burdens[^5].

I leave my testimony that as we come unto the Lord in His holy house and keep our sacred promises, we will feel His love guiding every step of our journey. In the name of Jesus Christ, amen.

## Notes

[^1]: See Mosiah 18:8–10 regarding the baptismal covenant to mourn with those that mourn and comfort those that stand in need of comfort.
[^2]: Gerrit W. Gong, “Come to the Lord in His Holy House,” General Conference, Oct. 2026.
[^3]: Alma 37:6–7.
[^4]: Russell M. Nelson, “Overcome the World and Find Rest,” Liahona, Nov. 2022, 95–98.
[^5]: See Matthew 11:28–30: "Take my yoke upon you, and learn of me... For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light."
